ARTISTS EXHIBITIONS ABOUT CONTACT

SOLO EXHIBITION

Mince comme des ailes de cigale

Pocono Zhao Yu

18 July — 17 August 2024

Curated by Li Bo Yan

Exhibition

Lightness always manifests itself in fragile, marginal and silent ways. In ancient China, cicadas were carved from jade and placed in the mouths of the deceased as symbols of rebirth. Each summer, cicadas appear to be “reborn” through moulting, although this transformation is not a resurrection in the literal sense.

Cicada slough is also an important ingredient in traditional Chinese medicine, historically associated with healing, while the delicate wings of the cicada have been used poetically to describe exceptionally fine and lightweight textiles. The jade cicada evokes rebirth, its slough evokes healing, and its wings suggest delicacy and abundance.

Beginning from this Eastern cultural perspective, Chinese artist Pocono Zhao Yu uses the image of the cicada to establish connections with contemporary reality. Her work frequently engages with human migration, conflicts between civilizations and environmental issues, using different media to weave together a network of contemporary human dilemmas.

In Terre-Patrie, Edgar Morin and Anne-Brigitte Kern use the term “polycrisis” to describe a condition in which major problems no longer appear in isolation, but overlap and intensify one another. Responding to contemporary conflicts, crises and uncontrollable events, Zhao Yu gathers images and texts and places them into complex relationships. Lost, forgotten and appropriated cultural remains repeatedly become points of departure for her observations.

In Mince comme des ailes de cigale, vanished historical landscapes appear on the walls like newly discovered archaeological sites, suggesting realities that have been obscured. Lightweight veils resembling flags are combined with painting, calligraphy and ceramics, evoking the slow process of healing.

A video work recording the sound of cicadas is treated through fragmentation. On the surface, the incessant chirping resonates with the heat of summer, yet these continuous “cries” suggest something deeper. Together, the works bear witness to the fragility of history and to the continual transformation of memory.
