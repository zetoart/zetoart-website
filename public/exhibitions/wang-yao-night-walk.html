SOLO EXHIBITION

Night Walk

Wang Yao

6 February — 1 March 2025

Exhibition

Night Walk presents Wang Yao’s recent oil paintings. The title originates from a new series, Night Walk Tour with AKO, but the idea of the night walk extends beyond the works themselves. It describes a state of being — moving through the night with a fragmented awareness, a sense of secrecy and an intimate curiosity toward the world.

Night gives things another dimension. During the day, actions and thoughts are often shaped by social rules and conventions. After dark, these structures loosen: boundaries become blurred, perception turns more subjective, and movement through space also becomes a movement of consciousness and sensation. Looking and being looked at, reality and illusion, fear and excitement begin to overlap.

Two years after her previous solo exhibition at ZETO ART, in which people close to her and her Shiba Inu occupied a central place, familiar figures return alongside other nocturnal creatures — mosquitoes, hedgehogs, cats and other animals that become active when daylight disappears.

These creatures act as silent witnesses to the night. Their gaze resembles that of the nocturnal wanderer: curious, tentative and observant. Night is therefore not exclusively a human experience, but a shared territory in which different beings move, cross paths and become aware of one another. This animal perspective opens another way of seeing, shifting the viewer away from a purely human-centred experience.

In Wang Yao’s paintings, darkness is not merely a background but an expanding space in which emotions, memories and undefined visions drift freely. Night acts as a catalyst, destabilising objects and weakening ordinary perceptions of time and space. Memories of the past, fragments of reality and echoes of dreams coexist within the same pictorial world.

A flickering light in the darkness, mushrooms hidden in the shadows, blooming flowers, a rose held between hands and feet, or a cigarette burning on a bed — these scenes do not leave the viewer outside the image. Instead, they gradually draw us into their atmosphere.

Night becomes both a temporal dimension and a mental territory. In Wang Yao’s work, it functions not only as a visual language, but as a psychological atmosphere encompassing intimacy, solitude, desire and illusion. Before these dimly illuminated images, we may remember a street corner late at night, an aimless walk, or an emotion that remains difficult to name — and it is precisely within this experience that Night Walk takes shape.
