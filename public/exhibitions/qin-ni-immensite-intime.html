SOLO EXHIBITION

L’immensité intime

Qin Ni

13 March — 19 April 2025

Exhibition

In the eighth chapter of The Poetics of Space, Gaston Bachelard explores the expansion of space within human imagination and memory. Through poetic experience, physical space can exceed its measurable limits and become an inner, fluid and seemingly infinite territory.

Seen through Qin Ni’s work, this inner space is not an external dimension that can be measured, but an “intimate immensity.” Its vastness does not depend on physical scale, but on the projection of memory, emotion and imagination. A small object or a confined room may therefore open onto an unlimited world within the mind.

This idea also resonates with Borges’s The Library of Babel, where knowledge and memory are neither linear nor finite, but generate endless combinations and possibilities. Qin Ni develops a similar structure through her fictional system of classification, in which personal memories are catalogued and archived within her Wu You Museum — a space that is simultaneously enclosed and infinitely expandable.

Her work also constructs what might be called a poetic space, where past and future coexist within the present moment of imagination. Old rooms, boxes, drawers and collected objects become shelters for memory, spaces in which linear time seems temporarily suspended.

In her paintings on wood, images are arranged almost like fragments of text, inviting the viewer into a process of reading and interpretation. The use of the Dewey Decimal Classification system is not only a reference to systems of knowledge and archiving, but also a way of moving between order and disorder — echoing Borges’s infinite labyrinth of knowledge.

Through this network of codes, objects and archived memories, Qin Ni gives material form to the idea of intimate immensity. Space becomes not simply a physical container, but a generator of emotion and imagination, while her Wu You develops into a poetic world continuously suspended between order and disorder, structure and wandering.
