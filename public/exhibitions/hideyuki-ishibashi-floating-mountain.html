SOLO EXHIBITION

Floating Mountain, Whispering Ashes

Hideyuki Ishibashi

13 November — 13 December 2025

Curated by Paula Zeng

Exhibition

ZETO ART is delighted to present Floating Mountain, Whispering Ashes, the first exhibition collaboration with France-based Japanese artist Hideyuki Ishibashi.

Shown in Paris for the first time, the exhibition presents five works from the Chromophore series. Each one is created with pigments derived from plants gathered on five former mining sites in the Nord–Pas-de-Calais region, among them viper’s bugloss, Saint-Lucie cherry, horned poppy, cladonia, hawthorn, meadow sage and bracken. Alongside the photographs, five incense blends created with artisans and produced by La Maison Tempsens from these same plants accompany the exhibition.

The coalfield of northern France is now recognized as a cultural landscape, extending from Condé-sur-l’Escaut on the Belgian border to Enquin-lez-Guinegatte at the edge of the Artois hills. Spanning 120 kilometers, this once-rural territory reveals a striking diversity of environments shaped by three centuries of coal extraction. Once markers of a vanished industrial era and powerful symbols of extraction and energy, these slag heaps have since shifted into emblematic figures of the Anthropocene. Today, they shape a collective visual memory of northern France, standing between working-class history and the quiet resurgence of the natural world.

Ishibashi first saw these dark silhouettes from a train heading toward Lille, their visual density leaving a deep impression. A decade later, commissioned by the CRP / Centre régional de la photographie Hauts-de-France, he returned. The mountains he found were no longer those of bare, industrial matter: plants now covered their flanks, and the palette had mellowed. The mountain had shifted from form to flux, becoming a terrain suspended between the artificial and the organic.

In his Chromophore series (2023), Ishibashi turns his attention to the materiality of the photographic medium. Working with the precision of a chemist, he combines scientific observation with artisanal gesture: infrared photography, the collection of plants and soil samples, the extraction of natural pigments in collaboration with color-designer Machiko Saito, and printing using the historical gum-bichromate process.

Each work takes on a distinct hue derived from plants gathered on the specific slag heap it depicts, revealing, beneath the surface of industrial history, an unexpected chromatic field. Far removed from the dark, mineral imagery traditionally associated with these landscapes, these prints are formed through layers of ash, dust, pigment, and air molecules that settle and interact over time. The image remains in constant transformation, a living material fluctuating between emergence and disappearance.

Within the exhibition space, the presence of scent extends the visual experience and draws the viewer’s body into a more expansive sensory encounter with the landscape. Positioned between industrial memory and vegetal regeneration, Ishibashi offers a meditation on transformation — on a world in flux and the quiet breathing of matter.
