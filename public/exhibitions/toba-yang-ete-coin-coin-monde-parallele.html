ARTISTS EXHIBITIONS ABOUT CONTACT

SOLO EXHIBITION

Été, coin-coin, monde parallèle

Toba Yang

13 April — 15 May 2024

Exhibition

Été, coin-coin, monde parallèle is Toba Yang’s first solo exhibition at the gallery. In this presentation, the artist unfolds a practice that moves fluidly across multiple supports, including oil painting, knitting, tapestry and digital drawing. The passage from one medium to another reflects the relaxed and comfortable state of mind in which she creates.

Through the three seemingly disparate words of the exhibition title — Summer, Coin-coin, and Parallel World — Toba Yang establishes subtle links between divergent worlds. The title also points directly to the three groups of works presented in the exhibition, forming the structure of a poetic narrative.

These bodies of work function like different dimensions of a poem: the setting of atmosphere (Summer), the unfolding of content (Parallel World), and even its punctuation (Coin-coin). Poetry, by nature, transgresses the limits of discourse and rhetoric, opening up possibilities beyond straightforward narration. It conveys emotional fullness through concise language and rhythmic cadence.

This sense of “transgression” is visible throughout Toba Yang’s work. What we encounter is not a fixed or precise form of expression, but rather an ambiguous and shifting feeling. Her works do not belong to one single story; instead, they appear as fragments that can be assembled into multiple narratives. They can feel warm, grotesque, mysterious, anxious or melancholic, yet they remain captivating in their beauty.

Toba often hides playful ideas within her images. In Standing on the sole of a giant’s foot, for example, a girl stands on the sole of a giant whose body seems upside down, transforming an oversized figure into something unexpectedly gentle and familiar. In The Will of the Round Leaves, the roots of the plant seem to possess a will of their own, even though they exist in a form that can easily go unnoticed.

The series Parallel Worlds suggests that lightness and heaviness coexist, and that choosing what one wants to be is a decision that belongs to each independent individual. Like the imagery of a poem, which constantly fluctuates, these works leave room for viewers to choose for themselves between melancholy and hope.
