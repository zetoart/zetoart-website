SOLO EXHIBITION

Earthly Fireworks

Huang Xiaoliang

10 October — 2 November 2024

Exhibition

Earthly Fireworks, in their fleeting brilliance, evoke both birth and disappearance, tracing a brief yet luminous passage between life and death. They illuminate the night sky only to dissolve with the arrival of dawn, carrying dreams and illusions with them. In Huang Xiaoliang’s work, fireworks become more than a physical phenomenon: they form an emotional symbol and a metaphor for existence.

The Earthly Fireworks series consists of more than ten small watercolours, each painted on a 10 × 10 cm sheet of paper. Despite their modest scale, these works open onto expansive imaginative spaces. Rather than directly representing reality, they reconstruct memory through fragments, filtering and condensing details of the visible world into dreamlike scenes.

These landscapes do not exist as such in the physical world, yet the emotions they convey remain strikingly real. Through these small images, Huang explores the complexity of personal memory while allowing it to resonate with a broader collective experience.

Alongside the watercolours, the exhibition presents a selection of photographic works. Rooted in documentary observation, Huang’s photographs capture fragments of everyday urban life, which he reorganises through a highly distinctive visual language. Rather than privileging clarity or faithful description, he reduces unnecessary detail and gives particular importance to atmosphere and the aura of the image.

Light plays a central role throughout both his photography and painting. Figures often appear as silhouettes that seem to belong to the environment while simultaneously dissolving into it, recalling the visual language of traditional Chinese shadow theatre. Light thus becomes a bridge between different spaces and times, between the visible and the invisible.

Photography and painting occupy two different positions in relation to reality. The photographs begin with observed moments, while the paintings construct fictional fragments of memory. Together they establish a dialogue between the tangible and the imaginary, the documented and the dreamed.

Like fireworks themselves, Huang Xiaoliang’s images explore the tension between transience and permanence. Through light, colour and memory, the exhibition reflects on the fragility of lived experience and on our persistent desire to preserve what is already disappearing.
