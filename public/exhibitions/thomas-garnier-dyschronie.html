ARTISTS EXHIBITIONS ABOUT CONTACT

SOLO EXHIBITION

Dyschronie

Thomas Garnier

7 October — 4 November 2023

Exhibition

Imagine gutted server racks, countless data centres, crypto-mining farms, logistics hubs and distribution centres lost in time. It becomes difficult to tell where vegetation ends and machines begin, where Ethernet cables intertwine with vines, and where roots seem to extend everywhere, connecting everything.

Among these imposing monoliths and hybrid structures, draped figures cross paths and embrace amid moss and mushrooms. Some sit or kneel in silence, absorbed in fervent contemplation or communion with an invisible entity. Are they waiting for its return, or is it already everywhere around us — flourishing within silicon micro-components and regenerating itself in every blade of grass?

Dyschronie brings together a series of works created by French artist Thomas Garnier over the previous three years, offering an insight into his exploration of alternative space-times and the ways in which they resonate with the present.

Garnier’s practice is characterised by an interdisciplinary approach that combines contemporary technologies such as photogrammetry, 3D printing and algorithmic image generation with older modes of representation including engraving, lithophane and rear projection.

This encounter between technological systems and historical forms produces a sensitive and deliberately anachronistic way of approaching the subjects represented — an ambiguous nostalgia for futures that have never existed.

Dyschronia is a disturbance in the perception and understanding of time, often characterised by a confusion between past and present and an inability to conceptualise or imagine the future: a literal “No Future”.

Garnier considers this notion in relation to Mark Fisher’s writings on hauntology, a concept derived from Jacques Derrida. Technologies that were once imagined as forces capable of breaking with the ghosts and cultural residues of the past seem, paradoxically, to intensify and accelerate their return within the collective imagination.

Text by Zeng Ye
