ARTISTS EXHIBITIONS ABOUT CONTACT

GROUP EXHIBITION

Sa patience de supporter une âme

4 July — 12 September 2026

Exhibition Introduction

The title of the exhibition, Sa patience de supporter une âme, is borrowed from a sentence in Rainer Maria Rilke’s The Notebooks of Malte Laurids Brigge. It may be understood as “the patience to bear a soul.” Rather than referring to the “soul” in a strictly religious or psychological sense, the phrase suggests a way of looking: what kind of image, material, landscape, or body can hold experiences that cannot be directly spoken?

The exhibition brings together five artists: Yorgos Yatromanolakis, Ren Han, Sun Yanchu, Xing Lei, and Dong Jiayuan. Their works span different media, including photography, silver gelatin painting, carved bamboo screen, and charcoal drawing. Yet they all engage with the idea of apparition: how an image slowly emerges from darkness, grain, light, material, or memory; how a body, a landscape, or a trace may be preserved within an image.

The exhibition does not attempt to depict the “soul” directly. Instead, it focuses on the things that may carry it: night, the surface of the sea, clouds, trees, light, the outline of a body, and the material surface of the image itself. In these works, figures are often not presented as clear subjects, but as presences suspended between appearance and disappearance. Landscapes are not merely natural scenes, but become vessels for memory, time, and interior experience.

About the Works and Artists

The work by Greek artist Yorgos Yatromanolakis comes from the series The Splitting of the Chrysalis & the Slow Unfolding of the Wings. In the image, a luminous human figure appears in a nocturnal wilderness. The figure seems at once to leap and to fall; the body is not fixed within a stable reality, but remains in a state of transition. The starry sky, rocks, and low-lying landscape form a quiet and mysterious field, where human presence appears fragile and fleeting, yet momentarily held by the night. The “light” in the work is not simply illumination, but a brief apparition between body and world.

Sun Yanchu’s Light of the Sea No. 6 continues his long-standing exploration of the relationship between photography, painting, and the process of development. Through silver gelatin painting, the work presents a beam of light on the sea: light descends from afar and leaves a bright trace across the surface of the water. Set within a black wooden panel, the image appears like a window opening from darkness. Sun’s work is not a landscape painting in the traditional sense, but rather a reflection on how an image comes into being. Silver gelatin, aluminium panel, and wood together create a material sense of time, as if the image were not painted, but slowly developed within the material itself.

Ren Han’s Untitled (23e03) is a carved bamboo screen. On a black bamboo surface, the artist carves a cloud-like form. A cloud has no fixed shape; it gathers, folds, changes, and resists definition. In this work, the cloud appears both as a natural phenomenon and as the shape of thought, breath, or consciousness. The bamboo screen, as a spatial material, transforms the work from a flat image into a presence on a bodily scale. To stand before it is not only to look at a form, but to encounter a slowly unfolding mental space.

Xing Lei’s photographic work There Are was made in 2015. In the image, three figures stand in the sea, facing away from the viewer and looking toward the distant horizon. The photograph has a strong grainy texture; the boundary between sky and sea becomes blurred, while the figures remain quiet dark silhouettes. There is no obvious event, nor any dramatic gesture, but precisely this stillness gives the work its force. It does not show “what happened,” but rather suggests that “there are” things present: people, sea, light, waiting, and a shared experience that remains difficult to name.

Dong Jiayuan’s Along the Way 1-2-3 consists of three drawings made with charcoal powder and charcoal stick. Trees, electric wires, slopes, and a dark sky can be discerned in the images, yet these elements are surrounded by dense grey and black particles, as if they might be swallowed again by darkness at any moment. Her works retain the most essential qualities of drawing: trace, friction, erasure, and accumulation. Rather than simply depicting landscapes, they record the process through which landscapes gradually become memory. The trees and wires appear like fragments encountered along the way, or like impressions that have become blurred but have not disappeared.

How to View the Exhibition

The works of these five artists are not simply juxtaposed by medium or geography. Together, they point toward a slower experience of looking: the image does not immediately deliver meaning, but gradually unfolds through the viewer’s attention.

Yorgos’s night, Sun Yanchu’s sea light, Ren Han’s cloud, Xing Lei’s figures in the water, and Dong Jiayuan’s trees and electric wires are not clear narrative scenes, but states of presence. They remind us that an image is not always there to explain something; sometimes it exists to preserve a presence that language has not yet fully captured.

In this sense, the “patience” of the exhibition is not only the patience of the artist, but also that of the material, the image, and the viewer. The grain of photography, the development of silver gelatin, the carving of bamboo, and the charcoal on paper all require time in order to be truly seen. What they bear is not a “soul” that can be easily named, but the fragile experiences of life that appear, disappear, and leave traces in the world.

Sa patience de supporter une âme invites the viewer to slow down. In entering these works, we do not simply see night, sea, clouds, and trees. We encounter the ways in which the world, in its own silence, bears and preserves what is fragile, invisible, and difficult to name.
